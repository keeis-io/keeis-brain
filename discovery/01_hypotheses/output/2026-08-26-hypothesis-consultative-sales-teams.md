# Customer hypothesis — high-ticket consultative sales teams in Costa Rica

- **Segment**: Costa Rican companies with consultative, high-ticket sales, commission-based reps,
  between ten and fifty salespeople on payroll, where the in-room conversation decides the sale.
  Verticals named: heavy machinery, medical equipment, kitchens, real estate (own developed
  projects), automotive.
- **Pain**: commercial managers of in-person sales teams don't actually know what happens inside
  client conversations. They supervise by the month's result, not by process, because
  accompanying every rep to every meeting is materially impossible once they have ten or more
  people reporting to them. Two consequences: lost sales can't be diagnosed, and a new rep takes
  months to reach the rest of the team's level because nobody can correct them on what they did
  wrong.
- **Current alternative**: not explicit in the hypothesis draft itself. From Mauricio Castro's
  interview (see `../../02_interviews/output/2026-08-26-interview-mauricio-castro.md`): the
  manager attends only "difficult" negotiations, pre-coordinated with the client, and the team
  gets standardized closing-technique training. No systematic review of every sales conversation.
  [Inference from one interview — not yet confirmed segment-wide.]
