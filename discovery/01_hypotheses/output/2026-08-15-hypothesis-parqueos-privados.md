# Customer hypothesis — private parking lot managers (GAM)

- **Segment**: operators/managers of private paid parking lots in the Greater Metropolitan Area
  (GAM) of Costa Rica — not condos, not security firms, not dealerships. One segment per run
  (`discovery/CONTEXT.md`); those three are logged below as backlog candidates for future runs.
- **Pain**: entry/exit today depends on a person reading and typing/logging plates (barrier
  attendant, ticket, or manual list) — slow at peak hours, error-prone, and gives no reliable
  digital record of which plate came in/out and when.
- **Current alternative**: a person at the booth with a ticket machine and/or a handwritten or
  spreadsheet log; some lots use RFID stickers/tags bought from the barrier vendor.
  [PENDING: confirm actual mix once interviews start — this is an assumption, not observed data]
- **Why they'd pay**: fewer attendants needed per shift, faster throughput at entry/exit, and an
  automatic digital log of every plate in/out (useful for billing disputes, lost-ticket claims,
  and theft/liability incidents).
- **Reference price**: [PENDING — ask what they currently pay for attendants per shift and/or for
  RFID tags/barrier systems, so the plates system has a real number to beat]
- **Riskiest assumption**: that a private lot operator would switch from (or add onto) an existing
  barrier/RFID setup for plate recognition specifically — vs. treating this as "not broken enough
  to change." If lots are already satisfied with RFID/tickets, this segment dies.
- **Test**: at least 3 of 8 interviewees describe this exact pain (slow entry, attendant cost, or
  no digital log) without us suggesting it first, AND they are currently paying something (labor,
  RFID hardware, or a service contract) to solve it today.
- **Skin-in-the-game evidence** (Savoia, *The Right It* — same book Erik recommended on 2026-08-12,
  see `../../../meetings/02_notes/output/2026-08-12-notes-erik.md`): committed time (a scheduled
  on-site visit to test the model against their real plates/lighting/angles), a letter of intent,
  or a small paid PoC ($2–5K, per Erik's benchmark for what investors treat as real traction) —
  not just a polite "sounds interesting."
