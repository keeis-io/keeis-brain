# Market and opportunity — informal microenterprises in Costa Rica

Supporting desk research for
`../output/2026-08-16-hypothesis-formalizacion-microempresas.md`. Captured 2026-08-16, sourced
from public market data (INEC, SINPE, Hacienda/TRIBU-CR, tech-adoption studies in SMEs).

## The segment

Informal microentrepreneurs or those transitioning to formality in Costa Rica — market vendors,
sodas, neighborhood hair salons, artisans, workshops — regardless of industry. Not the
already-formalized SME segment with an accountant, which is saturated with competition.

## Why there's a real gap (not a hunch)

- INEC counts 435,000+ household microenterprises in Costa Rica; 80.1% keep no formal
  accounting records.
- 62% of Costa Rican SMEs manage invoicing, inventory, and customer service manually or in
  Excel.
- An Alegra study (July 2026) found 45.5% of Costa Rican SMEs operate at a "basic" automation
  level, rising to 59.1% when including "very low." Unlike other regional markets (Colombia
  prioritizes time savings, Mexico prioritizes productivity), what Costa Rican business owners
  prioritize most is **control** — exactly what's least resolved for them today.

## The regulatory trigger (why now, not before)

- Since October 2025, e-invoicing v4.4 lets Hacienda pre-fill VAT declarations and audit in
  real time whether the rate charged matches the good sold.
- Failing to issue valid receipts can cost a fine of ₡924,400 (two base salaries) plus
  temporary closure on repeat offense.
- Hacienda's free e-invoicing tool can now only be used by independent professionals and
  accredited micro/small businesses — the government itself is pushing this segment toward
  private solutions.
- **July 2026**: reports indicate Hacienda began tracking whether payments received via SINPE
  Móvil correspond to undeclared economic activity, cross-checking the payment against the
  obligation to declare it. This makes the problem more urgent than it was a few months ago.

## The payment rail already in use

- Second half of 2024: 76% of Costa Ricans over 15 were already active SINPE Móvil users.
- In 2025, SINPE moved roughly ₡12.5 trillion across more than 747 million transfers (versus
  ₡10.9 trillion in 2024). It's already the de facto daily collection channel for sodas,
  workshops, market vendors, and independent professionals — not a card gateway.

## Conclusion

Any product for this segment must be born integrated with SINPE Móvil, not bolted on after the
fact. The combination of a legal obligation with a concrete fine + a payment channel already
adopted by 76% of the country + a gap in tools designed for the informal segment is the real
opportunity, not a hypothetical one.
