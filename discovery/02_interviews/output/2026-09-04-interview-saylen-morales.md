## Mentioned pains

- **No field CRM → delayed quotes and information loss risk** — stated directly and unprompted.
  The vendor must write notes wherever available (notebook, phone), return to the office, and
  reconstruct what the client needed before building a quote. Saylen named this explicitly as "una
  debilidad que tenemos como empresa." She also connected it to traceability: "no tenemos nada
  para medir ese tipo de interacciones del vendedor con el cliente."
- **Manager visibility gap** — Saylen cannot know what happened in a field visit except through
  verbal report or asking for notes. "No puedo más que conversando o pidiéndole las notas." She
  confirmed this is hard with 11 people.
- **Follow-up traceability gap** — If a vendor sent a follow-up email, Saylen has no way to know
  unless she was CC'd. Same for calls. The only way is to proactively ask the vendor on a specific
  deal. "No hay historiales de seguimiento porque esas cosas no quedan grabadas."
- **Lost sales with no diagnosis (private sector)** — In licitaciones, public data allows partial
  analysis (offers, specifications, price). In the private sector, the information is hermetic,
  and Saylen cannot determine whether a loss was due to price, advisory quality, or lack of
  follow-up. "A veces lo que uno no sabe es si fue que faltó seguimiento."
- **Quote assembly time as a loss driver** — Saylen named time to convert gathered client needs
  into a quote as a reason clients are lost, not a failure to understand the needs themselves.

## Contradictions / nuances vs. the hypothesis

**Positive for the hypothesis:**

- **Field content gap confirmed directly and unprompted** — vendors use physical notes and phones
  during visits, transcribe at the office. Identical structural gap to Salesforce at Purdy and
  the unnamed CRM at Disrupt. Now confirmed across all four data points.
- **Manager visibility gap confirmed** — Saylen has no access to what happens in visits except
  verbal report or notes. She named it a company weakness without being prompted. No other
  interview produced this level of directness on the visibility pain.
- **Follow-up traceability gap confirmed** — explicit: follow-up history is not recorded. She
  would need to be CC'd or proactively ask the vendor. Consistent with Pablo's framing.
- **Lost sales without diagnosis** — confirmed in private sector. Tally across interviews: 3
  confirm (Mauricio/Eurohogar, Pablo/Disrupt, Saylen/ENHMED), 1 denies (Carlos/Purdy).
- **Individual correction confirmed (Q4 fourth vote)** — Saylen does 1-on-1 coaching ("tú a tú")
  plus group formal training. Running Q4 tally: Mauricio (group-only), Carlos (individual
  in-branch), Pablo (market-based, no conversation-level individual), Saylen (individual coaching
  + group). Split now 2–2. Caveat: none of the four correct on conversation *content* because none
  of them have access to it — the gap is universal, but the appetite to fill it is unconfirmed.

**Complicating for the hypothesis:**

- **Licitación model challenges the hypothesis's core frame** — The hypothesis claims "the in-room
  conversation decides the sale." In ENHMED, 70–80% of revenue goes through public tenders where
  procurement committees compare specifications and price — not a single relationship conversation.
  The consultative interaction still matters (shaping the specification, building the relationship
  with the evaluating team), but the "conversation decides the close" claim does not hold cleanly
  in this vertical. This is the segment characteristic the guide flagged in advance.
- **The pain is operational (field capture, quote delay), not sales-intelligence (coaching,
  diagnosis)** — Saylen's spontaneous framing is a field data-capture problem: information gets
  lost or delayed before it becomes a quote. She did not frame it as "I can't coach my reps
  because I don't know what was said" or "I can't diagnose why a sale was lost." The visibility
  and diagnosis framing emerged when Kendall introduced it; Saylen confirmed it, but it was not
  her initial frame.
- **Q6 (dinero) not executed** — fourth consecutive interview where training investment was
  confirmed but no figure obtained. The hypothesis remains without a price signal.
- **No closing commitment** — guide's cierre question was skipped. No commitment evidence
  generated.

**Net reading of this interview against the hypothesis:**

The operational pain (field data loss, delayed quotes, manager blindness, follow-up traceability)
is the most cleanly confirmed pain across all four interviews — Saylen named it a company weakness
without prompting. What she describes is closer to a field CRM problem than a sales-coaching or
conversation-intelligence problem. The licitación model means the hypothesis's "conversation
decides the close" claim is a poor fit for this vertical. The coaching angle (the riskiest
assumption) remains unconfirmed as a buyer motivation: Saylen does individual coaching, but she
did not express a desire to do conversation-level coaching, and the question was never surfaced
clearly enough to generate that data.
