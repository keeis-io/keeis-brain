## 1. Mauricio Castro — Eurohogar

**Date**: 2026-08-26 (email exchange, ~1.5 hour turnaround)
**Participants**: Mauricio Castro Ulloa (commercial manager, Eurohogar); Kendall Fonseca
**Background**: Manages 14 sales reps across 8+ active real-estate projects.

**Current solution**: No named tool. Attends "difficult" closing negotiations in person
(pre-coordinated with the client); relies on standardized closing-technique training for the rest.

**What they like about the current solution**
- Direct presence at high-stakes negotiations gives first-hand visibility — satisfied, but it
  covers only a fraction of closings.

**Problems with the current solution**
- Job: know what happened in every closing conversation. Outcome wanted: catch what's going wrong
  without being physically present. Importance: high. Satisfaction: low — structurally can't
  attend all of them across 14 reps.
- Job: get a new rep productive fast. Outcome wanted: shorter ramp-up. Importance: medium-high.
  Satisfaction: neutral — ~6 months is treated as normal, not as a crisis.

**Key insights**
- *"Si me pudiese servir, pero no podría escuchar todas las reuniones de cierre de 14 vendedores"*
  — interest capped by a stated capacity objection, not enthusiasm.
- Weakest data point of the four: a three-question email reply, not a live interview.

**Action items**
- 2026-09-04 · Kendall/Isaac · Re-contact if a pilot proposal materializes. No commitment obtained.

## 2. Carlos Salazar — Purdy (Pérez Zeledón)

**Date**: 2026-08-27 (voice notes) + undated written answers
**Participants**: Carlos Salazar (manager, Purdy — Pérez Zeledón); relayed partly via Isaac Alvarado
**Background**: 5 sales reps at the branch; automotive dealership (inferred, not stated directly).

**Current solution**: Salesforce — logs interactions, scheduled follow-ups, contact status per
client. Used daily on desktop and phone.

**What they like about the current solution**
- Job: know whether a client has been contacted. Outcome wanted: clear pending/contacted status.
  Importance: high. Satisfaction: **high** — the panel gives exactly this.
- Job: onboard new reps. Importance: high. Satisfaction: satisfied with the current 3-month
  program (system training + product training + in-branch coaching).

**Problems with the current solution**
- Job: know what was actually said in a client conversation. Outcome wanted: call content, not
  just that it happened. Stated as fact (*"no queda la llamada completa"*) but **low urgency** — he
  has never had to dig into a missing conversation and has lost no sale to it.
- Job: control whether reps follow through. Outcome wanted: *"los dos flancos"* — a good system
  AND a control mechanism over the people. This is what he asked for when handed an opening.

**Key insights**
- The strongest disconfirming data point of the four. Asked directly whether he'd ever lost a sale
  to a missed or undocumented follow-up: no.
- Given the chance to name what he'd want, he asked for compliance enforcement, not conversational
  insight — a different product from the one being tested.

**Action items**
- 2026-09-04 · Kendall/Isaac · Open to re-contact (*"si no hay problema"*) — permission only, no
  commitment (no data, no pilot, no meeting scheduled).

## 3. Pablo Ramirez — Disrupt (real estate)

**Date**: 2026-09-04 (full call, ~30 min)
**Participants**: Pablo Ramirez (gerente, Disrupt); Kendall Fonseca
**Background**: 9 sales executives. 22 years at a transnational networking company, then commercial
lead selling technology projects, then 8 years in real estate — all consultative sales.

**Current solution**: An unnamed CRM used daily by all 9 reps — his *"termómetro"*: stage, close
probability, appointment history, objections and how each was resolved. Plus a separate scorecard
(*cuadro integral de mandos*, possibly Excel).

**What they like about the current solution**
- Job: measure whether a deal will close. Importance: high. Satisfaction: **high** — calls the CRM
  his primary management instrument.
- Job: manage without becoming a bottleneck. Outcome wanted: reps empowered to decide on the spot
  (discounts, extras) instead of escalating. Importance: high — an explicit management philosophy
  (empowerment vs. micromanagement).

**Problems with the current solution**
- Job: understand why a seemingly-won deal was lost. Importance: high — confirmed unprompted and
  repeatedly (*"sigue pasando... pasa más frecuente de lo que uno quisiera"*). Satisfaction: low.
- Job: surface the real, often hidden decision-maker before investing in the wrong pitch. Outcome
  wanted: pull all stakeholders in early (*"jalar más sombreros"*). Named as a generalizable skill
  gap, not an individual failing. Satisfaction: low — nothing helps with this today.

**Key insights**
- Confirms the content gap exists (the CRM stores notes, not the conversation) but does **not**
  treat it as a pain in itself. Three principled objections to solving it by recording: loss of
  spontaneity, legal/privacy exposure, and conflict with his empowerment model.
- Unprompted redirect at the close: wants *"agentes de IA entrenados en nuestro nicho"* for a
  focused problem — explicitly not a recording tool and not generic chat.
- *"Sería como tener una cámara y un micrófono encendido... por supuesto que sería muy valioso"* —
  immediately followed by the objections. Values the insight, resists the mechanism.

**Action items**
- 2026-09-04 · Kendall/Isaac · Re-contact when the product has concrete shape — stated twice,
  conditioned on *"elementos de valor"* being demonstrated first. No data, no intro, no commitment.

## 4. Saylen Morales — ENHMED (lab equipment)

**Date**: 2026-09-04 (full call)
**Participants**: Saylen Morales (jefa de ventas, ENHMED); Kendall Fonseca
**Background**: 11 sales reps; distributor of laboratory equipment; 70–80% of sales via public
tender (licitación), producing a long, multi-touch cycle (phone, email, in-person).

**Current solution**: **No CRM.** An ERP for internal data, not portable to the field. Reps write
client needs on paper, phone, or a notebook, then reconstruct the offer back at the office.
Technical product information lives on a company server.

**What they like about the current solution**: Not stated. Nothing in the interview surfaces an
element of the current process she is satisfied with; the workflow reads entirely as a workaround.

**Problems with the current solution**
- Job: capture client requirements accurately and fast. Outcome wanted: nothing lost between visit
  and office. Importance: high. Satisfaction: low — named explicitly as a company weakness
  (*"eso sí es como una debilidad que tenemos"*).
- Job: know what happened in a rep's visit she wasn't part of. Outcome wanted: visibility beyond
  verbal reporting. Importance: high — she personally steps into negotiations *"bastantes veces"*.
  Satisfaction: low — *"no puedo más que conversando o pidiéndole las notas"*.
- Job: understand why a bid was lost to a competitor. Importance: high — confirmed
  (*"no tenemos la información de qué fue lo que pasó"*). Partly diagnosable for public tenders
  (bid data is public); not for private-sector losses.
- Job: ramp up new reps faster. Importance: medium — handled today via 1:1 coaching with her and
  senior reps; effective but slow (6–12 months).

**Key insights**
- 70–80% via licitación is a segment-defining fact absent from the original hypothesis: much of
  this pain routes through public-bid dynamics, not pure 1:1 consultative conversation.
- For the non-tender deals she attributes high influence to the conversation itself: visits
  surface upsell opportunities the original request never mentioned.
- **Protocol gap** (already flagged in the structured interview): the closing commitment question
  was never asked; the session ended on pleasantries.

## Which profile to pilot first

Ranking the four by what matters for a *first* pilot — unsatisfied job, absence of an incumbent
occupying the workflow, and whether the manager already invests in 1:1 coaching:

| | Unsatisfied job | Incumbent in the workflow | Already invests in 1:1 coaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mauricio (Eurohogar) | Partial | None named | No — group training only |
| Carlos (Purdy) | **No** — satisfied | Salesforce, satisfied | Yes |
| Pablo (Disrupt) | Yes | CRM, satisfied | No — quarterly market-based correction |
| **Saylen (ENHMED)** | **Yes, named as a weakness** | **None — paper and phone** | **Yes** |

ENHMED is the only one with all three, which makes it the strongest **first** pilot candidate —
the one where a negative result is most informative, because nothing else is occupying the
workflow to blame it on.

That is a sequencing call, not a segment restriction. Purdy and Disrupt are not out of the
segment; they are profiles where the offer would have to beat an incumbent CRM, which is a harder
first test, not an impossible one. The plan's Phase 4 and 5 require piloting **at least two
distinct sales profiles** before any decision — so a second profile gets its own pass, and
comparing the two is part of the evidence.
