## Which profile this addresses — and what it does NOT mean

The template takes one profile per pass. Chosen here: the **Saylen Morales / ENHMED profile** —
technical consultative B2B distribution, ~10 reps, **no CRM occupying the field-visit workflow**.

Rationale for going first with this one: it is the only one of the four interviews where the pain
is structural *and* there is no incumbent tool. Carlos Salazar (Purdy) runs Salesforce and Pablo
Ramirez (Disrupt) runs a CRM he calls his "termómetro" — both are satisfied with it, so any offer
there competes against a paid tool the manager already finds adequate. ENHMED has nothing: reps
write client requirements on paper, a notebook, or their phone.

> **This does not narrow the hypothesis.** The segment under validation remains what
> `../../01_hypotheses/output/2026-08-26-hypothesis-consultative-sales-teams.md` states:
> Costa Rican companies with consultative, high-ticket sales. The whole point of the pilot channel
> is to keep validating that segment **more realistically** — with companies actually running the
> thing — rather than to shrink it to one vertical. The field plan requires the opposite:
> Phase 4 repeats across 2–3 more companies, and Phase 5 will not accept a decision built on
> fewer than **two distinct sales profiles**.
>
> So this document is *the value proposition for the first profile we pilot*, not *the value
> proposition of the company*. A second profile (hybrid high-ticket sales with few reps, per the
> plan's Route B priority) will need its own pass — value propositions are profile-specific by
> construction, and the differences between them are themselves evidence.

## 1. Who

Sales managers at technical B2B distributors in Costa Rica (lab, medical, industrial equipment),
8–15 reps, high ticket, long cycle. Characteristics: reps run in-person technical advisory visits;
an administrative ERP exists but **no field CRM**; the manager already invests in 1:1 coaching and
technical training; holds pricing/discount authority for their area.

*Evidence: Saylen Morales, ENHMED — 11 reps, 70–80% of sales via licitación, "tú a tú" coaching,
decides pricing and discounts alone.*

## 2. Why — problem and JTBD

**JTBD**: "When my rep comes back from a visit, I need to know what actually happened there, so I
can help them win the deal and correct them before it's lost — without going to every visit
myself."

Desired outcomes, all with direct evidence:
- Technical requirements the rep gathers don't get lost between the visit and the quote —
  *"si hayan usado un papelito, ahí sí se le puede perder por completo la información."*
- Being able to measure how effective a rep's visit was —
  *"ahorita no tenemos nada para medir ese tipo de interacciones del vendedor con el cliente."*
- Understanding why a deal was lost to a competitor —
  *"nos damos cuenta que ganó la competencia y tal vez no tenemos la información de qué fue lo que pasó."*

## 3. What before — current situation

The rep writes notes in a notebook, on their phone, or on paper during the visit. Back at the
office they reconstruct what the client asked for and assemble the quote. The ERP stores
administrative data, not the conversation. The manager's only route to knowing what happened is to
ask or request the notes — *"no puedo más que conversando o pidiéndole las notas."*

Concrete friction: **time-to-quote is the bottleneck and has already cost deals** —
*"a veces por el tiempo que tomamos en poder juntar toda la necesidad y convertirla en una oferta."*
With 11 people, traceability is acknowledged as impossible (*"sí es difícil"*) and named by the
manager herself as a company weakness.

## 4. How — the solution

**Form chosen: a delivered analysis service, not a supervision tool.** The field conversation is
captured, and the manager receives **a pre-digested, actionable analysis — the manager listens to
nothing, reviews no recordings, spends no hours.**

This is the direct answer to the three objections raised by Pablo Ramirez
(`../../02_interviews/output/2026-09-04-interview-pablo-ramirez.md`), which is why the form matters:

| Objection | How this form answers it |
|---|---|
| Micromanagement vs. empowerment — the manager is a finite resource | The manager never enters the review loop; they receive conclusions. Their time is not consumed. |
| Spontaneity — reps and clients self-censor when recorded | The analysis targets what the client needed and what got lost in translation, not auditing the rep's script. |
| Legal / privacy | Explicit consent from both parties designed in, not patched on. **[PENDING — unresolved blocker]** |

In the pilot this is **manual** (concierge MVP): we analyze, we deliver. Nothing to install,
nothing to integrate, no change to the rep's workflow beyond recording.

## 5. What after — future state

- The manager sees, for the first time, what was discussed in visits they didn't attend — without
  attending and without listening.
- The client's technical requirement is captured intact rather than depending on a piece of paper.
  *(Reduces rework risk and shortens the path to a quote — magnitude [PENDING: not measured].)*
- The 1:1 coaching she already does stops resting on the rep's retelling and starts resting on
  what happened.
- On a loss to a competitor, there is material to tell whether it was price, follow-up, or
  advisory quality — instead of speculation.
- *[PENDING: any reduction in the 6–12 month ramp-up for a new rep — hypothesis, not measured.]*

## 6. Alternatives

| Alternative | Why it doesn't do the job | Switching cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Do nothing** (paper + ask the rep) — ENHMED's status quo | Depends on the rep's retelling; information gets lost; nothing measurable | Zero — this is the real rival |
| **Buy a CRM** (Salesforce et al.) | Records *that* an interaction happened plus typed notes, **not what was said** — confirmed at Purdy and at Disrupt | High: license + adoption, and capture stays manual |
| **Manager attends the visits** | She already does, "bastantes veces" — doesn't scale to 11 people; per Pablo, a selling manager is expensive for the organization | Her time, the scarce resource |
| **More group training** | Teaches general technique, doesn't correct what happened in a specific visit | Already doing it; doesn't close the visibility gap |
| **Record and have the manager listen** | Returns the finite-resource problem — Pablo's objection | Her time, again |

**Why they'd choose us**: it is the only option that returns *interpreted conversation content*
without costing the manager time. And in the pilot, at no cost and no commitment — entry barrier
zero by design.

## What this closes and what it doesn't

- **Closes assumption #6 on paper** (see `2026-09-04-assumptions-prioritized.md`): the form is a
  *delivered analysis service*, not a supervision tool and not compliance control. Still to be
  validated in the field, but it is now an explicit, falsifiable bet.
- **Consistent with the plan's Phase 1.2 directive**: leads with field visibility and training
  acceleration, not with "preventing lost sales" (a pain not consistently confirmed).
- **Does not close assumption #5**: the end-client consent mechanism appears inside the "How" and
  remains unresolved. No formal proposal can be drafted until it is.
- **Does not answer the four open questions in the plan's Phase 1.1**: how many reps in the first
  pilot, where recordings are stored and with what access restriction, how long data is retained,
  and whether analysis is fully manual or transcription-assisted.
